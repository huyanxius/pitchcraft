# Competition Storyline and Timing

## Governing principle

Build for the judge's decision, not for completeness. Repository structure is not presentation structure.

## Default competition arc

Use and adapt this arc:

1. Hook: make the problem concrete and memorable.
2. Problem: show who suffers, where the friction occurs, and why it matters.
3. Insight: reveal why current approaches fail or why the timing has changed.
4. Product thesis: state the product's distinct answer.
5. Product workflow: demonstrate the smallest credible end-to-end path.
6. Proof: show working product, validation, technical feasibility, or user evidence.
7. Differentiation: explain the defensible product or execution advantage.
8. Path forward: show roadmap, adoption, business model, or scale logic without overstating proof.
9. Closing: restate the memorable judgment and desired next action.

Do not force every deck to use nine slides. Merge or split based on duration and evidence.

## Timing budget

Reserve time before assigning slide count:

- Opening and closing: 10-15% total.
- Product explanation and demonstration: 35-45% total.
- Problem, insight, proof, differentiation, and path forward: remaining time.
- Live demo buffer: add explicit transition and recovery time.

For ordinary spoken pages, target roughly 35-60 seconds. Use shorter transition pages sparingly. Do not solve timing by shrinking text.

## Slide job model

Every slide must answer:

- What should the judge believe after this page?
- What visible proof makes that belief credible?
- What will the speaker say that is not written on the page?
- Why must the next page follow?

If a slide has two unrelated spoken jobs, split it. If two consecutive slides repeat the same job, merge them.

## Title rules

Use conclusion-led titles:

- Prefer: `小团队缺的不是创意，而是把创意稳定送达多平台的基础设施`
- Avoid: `行业痛点`

The action title is the only highest-priority conclusion zone. Keep numbers inside the proof object rather than creating detached KPI titles.

```markdown
- <opening or thesis page>
- <evidence-heavy product page>
```

## Speaker-note rules

- Write natural spoken Chinese, not report prose.
- Give each slide one opening sentence, 2-4 explanation beats, and one transition.
- Keep the script within the assigned time at a realistic speaking pace.
- Mark `[DEMO]`, `[CLICK]`, `[PAUSE]`, and `[BACKUP]` cues explicitly.
- Do not make the speaker read the slide verbatim.
